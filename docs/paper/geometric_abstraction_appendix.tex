\section{Geometric abstraction implementation}
\label{app:geometric-abstraction}

The abstraction converts calibrated RGB-D into metric entities, symbolic
references, and compact text. It runs locally on the CPU, with fixed estimators
throughout policy training and online evaluation.

\paragraph{From pixels to metric geometry.}
Color masks associate visible pixels with objects. For a valid pixel
$\tilde{\mathbf u}=(u,v,1)^\top$, we compute its world position as
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf p_W=R_{WC}\bigl(D(u,v)K^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf u}\bigr)+\mathbf t_{WC},
\end{equation}
where $D$ is optical-axis depth, $K$ contains camera intrinsics, and
$(R_{WC},\mathbf t_{WC})$ maps camera to world coordinates. Background depth
estimates the table plane; public robot geometry supports robot masking.
Cuboid and cylinder observers fit pose and supported surfaces using dimensions
initialized from RGB-D under the declared shape and support assumptions.
Simulator object poses and dimensions are not inputs.

\paragraph{Points, boundaries, and openings.}
For polygonal extrusions, we fit a cap plane, project contour pixels onto it,
fit straight boundary segments, and intersect neighboring lines to obtain 3D
vertices. Ordered vertices define edges and planar faces. The Ring observer
retains separate outer and inner boundaries and requires deeper visible depth
inside the opening. It derives the opening center from the inner polygon's area
centroid, its axis from the cap normal, and a centered clearance radius from the
nearest inner edge. Thus the opening center is a derived geometric point;
sampling depth at that image location may instead return the background.
No six-vertex template is supplied. Initial table support and constant extrusion
determine thickness and the inferred bottom; the peg is fitted from its circular
cap. This observer assumes resolved convex boundaries and one convex opening.

\paragraph{References and current evidence.}
Names such as \texttt{red.top\_face} resolve to typed values: points, axes, or
faces with centers and normals. Records carry world coordinates, units,
observation times, and observability or inference metadata. Initialized shape
can persist, but a movable object's new pose requires current visual evidence.
For an occluded ring, the initialized boundaries are registered to current
visible edges; underconstrained or ambiguous fits return unavailable. Hidden
surfaces retain their model-derived labels. The fixed peg may retain an earlier
measurement with its original timestamp. Geometric support relations expose
residuals and unavailable evidence; they do not certify physical contact or
stability.

\paragraph{From geometry to agent input.}
Full receives selected object geometry, references, evidence status, and, where
requested, goal residuals as text. Dense boundary arrays are omitted from online
Ring feedback. Multiple queries can reuse one observation, while local sensing
can refresh geometry without attaching another image to the LLM. Initial and
requested images remain available. Generated policies use geometric queries
during rollouts; online agents use the updated text to choose their next action.

\paragraph{Illustrative decisions.}
For an upright cube, suppose \texttt{red.bottom\_face} is centered at
$(0.12,-0.01,0.08)$ and \texttt{blue.top\_face} at $(0.10,0.02,0.04)$ meters,
with opposing normals. Aligning them gives translation
$(-0.02,0.03,-0.04)$; the policy still chooses when and how to place the cube.
For Ring, the agent can compare the current opening center and axis with the
peg's top and axis before choosing a pad motion or another observation. An
unavailable opening estimate calls for renewed evidence rather than treating
the commanded gripper motion as a measured object pose.
